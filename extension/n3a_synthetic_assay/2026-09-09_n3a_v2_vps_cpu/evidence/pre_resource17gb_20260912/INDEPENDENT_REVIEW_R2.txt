## 4. Firewall defensibility and scope of support

Running A while implementing B is defensible. B's dataset, architecture, checkpoints, windows, seeds, direction, threshold and precision boundary are fixed in a hash-bound document that predates any A outcome. The remaining B freedoms, loader, token mask, initialization and sample order, are declared frozen before any A reading, and an unimplementable interface is a workflow failure rather than a re-selection. The analysis CLI refuses without a frozen B manifest, and every operational surface I read is outcome-blind: campaign stdout prints status, counts, elapsed and bytes; the supervisor terminal carries a null decision; heartbeats carry steps and counts; the receipt carries bytes, seconds and memory. The raw metric files remain readable on disk and are mirrored, so at that layer the guard is procedural, which condition 7 and the D1 repair make explicit. An unimplemented B is not transfer evidence, and A support can never substitute for it.

If A later returns BACKWARD_SUPPORTED_SYNTHETIC, the supportable statement is narrow: in this synthetic 32-position, top-8 routing task, with two small router architectures and fresh paired seeds, the assigned informative tail-direction surrogate lowered validation cross-entropy relative to a norm-matched permuted-direction null over 32 updates, late in training at densities above the selected support, alongside the registered sparse-versus-dense crossover context. It shows no real-data transfer, no mechanism behind benchmark reversals, no new method, no direction benefit independent of parameter step size, and no novelty for the P-minus-L placement contrast given prior tail-preservation work and generic straight-through estimators. FORWARD_ONLY is weaker still. INCONCLUSIVE or KILLED are reportable outcomes with no confirmatory B.
